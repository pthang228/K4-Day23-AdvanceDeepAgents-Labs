# World Models: From Latent Imagination to Interactive Generative Simulators

## TL;DR

- A world model is a learned, largely unsupervised predictor of environment dynamics: the original formulation trains a VAE encoder, an MDN-RNN latent dynamics model and a tiny controller, and shows a policy can be trained entirely inside the model's "dream" and transferred back to the real environment (CarRacing 906±21 vs. DQN 343±18) [1][2], while also exposing the central hazard that a controller can exploit model imperfections (τ = 0.10 gave imaginary 2086±140 but real 193±58) [2].
- The latent-imagination line — PlaNet's recurrent state-space model, Dreamer's analytic value gradients, DreamerV3's single-configuration generality and MuZero's reward/policy/value-only model — established data-efficient control without reconstructing pixels, but each is evaluated on reward benchmarks rather than physical fidelity [3][4][5][6][7][8].
- Since 2024 the field has scaled to internet-video-trained generative "world foundation models" and action-conditioned interactive engines: Sora's spacetime-patch diffusion transformer, Cosmos's diffusion 7B/14B and autoregressive 4B/12B family with 720p tokenizers, Genie's 11B parameter interactive environments, 20 FPS neural game engines (GameNGen, Oasis, MineWorld at 4–7 FPS), and Genie 3 at 720p/24 FPS with roughly one minute of visual memory [9][10][11][12][13][14][15][16].
- Evaluation evidence is convergent and unflattering on physics and long horizons: the best Physics-IQ model reaches 29.5% on a scale whose maximum possible score is 100.0% [17]; 83.3% of exocentric and 93.5% of egocentric generated videos contain at least one human-identifiable physical glitch [18]; and no interactive model passes all long-horizon dimensions, with the best PlayWorld overall score 2.12/3 [19][20][21].
- Model quality is now formally linked to a failure mode that accuracy metrics miss: exploitation is "essentially unavoidable on large policy sets", the guaranteed "safe horizon" shrinks as model error grows, and low prediction error does not preclude inversions of policy ordering [22]; scaling studies find out-of-distribution physical-law errors do not improve with data or parameters [23], and a position piece argues "a world model is NOT about generating videos" [24].

## Background

A world model, in the sense established by Ha and Schmidhuber, is a generative neural network model of a reinforcement learning environment, trained "quickly in an unsupervised manner to learn a compressed spatial and temporal representation of the environment"; the extracted features feed a compact policy that can be trained "entirely inside of its own hallucinated dream" and then transferred to the real environment [1]. The reference implementation decomposes the idea into three components: a VAE (V) compressing each frame into a latent vector (z ∈ R^32 in CarRacing, R^64 in VizDoom), an MDN-RNN (M) modelling P(z_{t+1} | a_t, z_t, h_t) as a mixture of Gaussians, and a linear controller (C) evolved with CMA-ES [2]. Its reported scores make the case quantitatively: the full model reached 906±21 on 100 random CarRacing tracks (the task is defined as solved at 900) versus 343±18 for DQN and 591–652 for A3C, and under the VizDoom setup the agent trained purely in imagination scored 1092±556 steps in the actual game against 210±108 for a random policy [2]. The same source documents the core risk that recurs throughout the later literature: at low temperature (τ = 0.10) the virtual score rose to 2086±140 while real performance fell to 193±58, and increasing τ "helps prevent our controller from taking advantage of the imperfections of our world model" [2].

The stakes are data efficiency and generality. DreamerV3 is reported to outperform specialized methods across over 150 diverse tasks "with a single configuration" and to be the first algorithm to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data or curricula [6]; that first diamond arrives at 30M environment steps, described as 17 days of playtime, with 24 of 40 seeds trained for 100M steps collecting at least one diamond and the best agent doing so in 6 episodes [7]. On Atari at 200M steps DreamerV3's median score is 302% against DreamerV2's 219%, and on 8 DMLab tasks it matches or exceeds IMPALA's final performance in 50M rather than 10B environment steps [7]. The motivation is strongest where real data is expensive: NVIDIA's Physical AI framing argues that "scaling training data for Physical AI is much more challenging, as the desired data must contain sequences of interleaved observations and actions" and exploratory actions "may cause severe damage", so a learned world model is proposed as a digital twin a Physical AI "can safely interact with" [25][10].

The term also carries a practical, heterogeneous meaning in current tooling. A Hub search for "world model" surfaces artifacts ranging from `thuml/webarena-world-model-rlvr` (2B) and `1x-technologies/worldmodel_unet_v0` (0.9B) to `X1AOX1A/WorldModel-Stabletoolbench-Llama3.1-8B` (8B) and robotics entries such as `agibot-world/EWMBench-model`, with parameter counts spanning 0.1B to 8B [26]; Hub Spaces include interactive demos ("Watch an AI plan and move in a grid world"), predictive-video demos (Cosmos, PROMETHEUS) and challenge leaderboards ("1X World Model Challenge", "GigaBrain Challenge 2026 World Model Track") [27]. Any survey therefore has to separate three distinct research objects that share the label: latent dynamics models used for control, generative video models used as simulators, and text/latent world-state models used inside agentic pipelines.

## Latent world models and imagination-based control

The most direct continuation of the V/M/C design keeps the encoder/latent-dynamics core but adds planning and value learning. PlaNet learns environment dynamics from images and chooses actions "through fast online planning in latent space", using a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) with both deterministic and stochastic transition components (h_t = f(h_{t-1}, s_{t-1}, a_{t-1}); s_t ~ p(s_t | h_t)) trained with a multi-step objective the authors call latent overshooting [3][4]. Evaluated on six image-based continuous control tasks from the DeepMind control suite with 64×64×3 observations, it used 10 to 20 hours on a single Nvidia V100, matched D4PG except on the finger task, exceeded it by a relative 26% on cheetah running, and did so "within less than one hundredth the episodes" (200× fewer) [4]. The authors' own stated limitation — "learning representations without reconstruction could help to solve tasks with higher visual diversity" — anticipates both the JEPA programme and the diffusion world models that followed [4].

Dreamer replaced planning with imagination: it learns behaviors "by propagating analytic gradients of learned state values back through trajectories imagined in the compact state space of a learned world model", reaching an average of 823 across 20 visual control tasks after 5×10^6 environment steps versus 332 for PlaNet and 786 for D4PG after 10^8 steps [5]. Its reported mechanism-limitation is that optimizing only imagined rewards within a fixed horizon "results in shortsighted behaviors", which the learned value model mitigates (better than alternatives on 16 of 20 tasks at horizon 20) [5]. MuZero takes the opposite tack of predicting less: its learned model, "when applied iteratively, predicts the quantities most directly relevant to planning: the reward, the action-selection policy, and the value function", achieving new state of the art on 57 Atari games and matching AlphaZero on Go, chess and shogi without being given the rules [8]. Backbone choice matters for long-horizon fidelity: the S4WM work states that RNN-based world models such as Dreamer's "have limited memory capacity", and reports that a structured state-space (S4-style) backbone "outperforms Transformer-based world models in terms of long-term memory, while exhibiting greater efficiency during training and imagination" [28].

The non-generative counter-current is the joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) family. I-JEPA predicts "the representations of various target blocks in the same image" from a single context block — a "non-generative approach for self-supervised learning from images" — and trains a ViT-Huge/14 on ImageNet using 16 A100 GPUs in under 72 hours [29]. LeCun's position paper motivates exactly this: societies "still lack Level 5 self-driving cars, domestic robots, and virtual assistants that learn reliable world models, reason, and plan complex action sequences", human learning is efficient enough that babies "quickly obtain an intuitive understanding of physics", whereas "robots trained through RL struggle with this task, requiring years of simulated interactions" [30]. Concrete instantiations now exist: V-JEPA is trained solely with a feature-prediction objective on 2 million videos and reaches, with a frozen backbone, 81.9% on Kinetics-400, 72.2% on Something-Something-v2 and 77.9% on ImageNet1K [31]; V-JEPA 2 is pre-trained on "over 1 million hours of internet video", reaches 77.3 top-1 on Something-Something v2 and 39.7 recall-at-5 on Epic-Kitchens-100, and adds an action-conditioned 300M-parameter transformer world model post-trained on "approximately 62 hours of unlabeled video from the raw Droid dataset" that is deployed zero-shot on Franka arms in two labs with image-goal planning and no task-specific reward [32][33]. The practical footprint of this line is visible on the Hub: `facebook/vjepa2-vitl-fpc64-256` is a 0.3B-parameter video model with 64-frame clips, 219,971 downloads in the last month, and `get_vision_features` returning video/image embeddings [34].

## Generative and interactive world models at scale

The 2024–2026 wave reframes world modelling as video generation or as real-time neural simulation. Sora is trained as a text-conditional diffusion transformer over "spacetime patches of video and image latent codes", can generate "a minute of high fidelity video" and samples "widescreen 1920x1080p videos, vertical 1080x1920 videos" with images up to 2048x2048; OpenAI claims capabilities such as 3D consistency, long-range coherence and even controlling a Minecraft player "emerge without any explicit inductive biases for 3D, objects, etc.—they are purely phenomena of scale", while stating that Sora "does not accurately model the physics of many basic interactions, like glass shattering" and that eating food "do[es] not always yield correct changes in object state"; no parameter count, dataset size or quantitative benchmark is provided [9]. Third-party survey reading of the same model is more cautious, noting Sora "can only passively generate video sequences based on an observed initial state, but cannot actively intervene" and "still fails to reproduce correct physical laws consistently" [35], while another survey preserves the affirmative reading, arguing that persistent painter strokes and bite marks "indicate that Sora can understand the world and predict the results of an action" [36].

The interactive branch is defined by throughput and action conditioning rather than by visual scale. Genie, "the first generative interactive environment trained in an unsupervised manner from unlabelled Internet videos", comprises a spatiotemporal video tokenizer, an autoregressive dynamics model and a latent action model, and at 11B parameters is described by its authors as "a foundation world model" that lets users act frame-by-frame without ground-truth action labels [12]. Genie 2 generates action-controllable playable 3D worlds from a single prompt image, with emergent object interactions, character animation and physics, though the blog reports no parameter count, frame rate or resolution [37]; Genie 3 navigates in real time at 24 FPS and retains consistency for a few minutes at 720p, and the blog's limitations section states that the model supports only "a few minutes of continuous interaction, rather than extended hours", that autoregressive generation makes inaccuracies accumulate, that the directly performable action space is limited, and that visual memory extends as far back as around one minute [16]. The public Project Genie prototype repeats the constraints: worlds "might not look completely true-to-life or always adhere closely to prompts or images, or real-world physics", character control suffers higher latency, and generations are limited to 60 seconds [38].

Neural game engines target real-time interaction with explicit action inputs. GameNGen is "the first game engine powered entirely by a neural model", runs at 20 frames per second on a single TPU, achieves a next-frame PSNR of 29.4 "comparable to lossy JPEG compression", and produces clips that human raters distinguish from the real game only slightly better than chance after 5 minutes of autoregressive generation [13]. Oasis conditions each frame on keyboard input with a ViT autoencoder plus DiT backbone trained using Diffusion Forcing, generates 20 FPS (one frame every 0.04 seconds, claimed "over 100x faster" than text-to-video models that need 10–20 seconds per second of video), and releases 500M-parameter open weights while noting that an order of magnitude more speed requires new hardware [14]. MineWorld interleaves discrete image and action tokens and trains with next-token prediction, using a parallel decoding algorithm to reach 4 to 7 frames per second, and reports outperforming open-source diffusion world models on new metrics that assess action-following as well as visual quality [15]. Dreamer 4 pushes in the opposite direction — a scalable agent learning "by reinforcement learning inside of a fast and accurate world model", with real-time interactive inference on a single GPU via shortcut forcing, and the claim of being "the first agent to obtain diamonds in Minecraft purely from offline data, without environment interaction" while issuing over 20,000 mouse and keyboard actions [39]. Diffusion is also used directly as a differentiable dynamics model: DIAMOND ("DIffusion As a Model Of eNvironment Dreams") reaches "a mean human normalized score of 1.46 on the competitive Atari 100k benchmark; a new best for agents trained entirely within a world model", motivated by the argument that compressing dynamics into discrete latents "may ignore visual details that are important for reinforcement learning" [40].

For physical AI the dominant industrial pattern is a pre-trained world foundation model that is fine-tuned into domain models. NVIDIA's Cosmos platform covers a video-curation pipeline, pre-trained world foundation models, post-training recipes and video tokenizers, with both diffusion and autoregressive transformers "trained using continuous and discrete latent representations of videos, respectively", plus camera-controllable, manipulation instruction-following and autonomous-driving variants, released open-weight under permissive licenses [10][25]. The released matrix quantifies the family: diffusion `Cosmos-Predict1-7B/14B-Text2World/Video2World` models and autoregressive 4B/12B (and 5B/13B video-to-world) models, paired with continuous tokenizers at 8×8×8 spatiotemporal compression with 121 frames of context and discrete tokenizers at 8×16×16 with 49 frames of context, up to 720p [11]. Cosmos-Predict2.5 is built on a flow-based architecture unifying Text2World, Image2World and Video2World, trained on 200M curated video clips with reinforcement-learning post-training, released at 2B and 14B scales, accompanied by Cosmos-Transfer2.5 — a control-net style framework for Sim2Real and Real2Real translation that is "3.5x smaller than Cosmos-Transfer1" yet reported to deliver higher fidelity and robust long-horizon generation [41]. In driving, GAIA-1 maps video, text and action inputs to discrete tokens and predicts the next token, with a 6.5B-parameter world model trained on 4,700 hours at 25Hz (roughly 420M unique images) using T=26 sequences at 6.25 Hz, 32 text tokens and 2 action tokens per step and a total sequence length of 15,860 tokens [42]; Wayve's follow-up GAIA-2 is a latent-diffusion model conditioned on ego dynamics, agent configurations, environmental factors and road semantics, generating spatiotemporally consistent multi-camera video across the UK, US and Germany [43]. The same conditioning discipline appears in the JEPA line, where V-JEPA 2-AC's 300M-parameter predictor is compared against generalist and world-model baselines on a Franka arm (Reach / Grasp Cup / Grasp Box / Pick-and-Place Cup / Pick-and-Place Box: V-JEPA 2-AC 100/60/20/80/50%, versus Octo 100/10/0/10/10% and Cosmos 80/0/20/0/0%), and Meta reports 65–80% pick-and-place success on new objects in unseen environments [44].

## How world models are actually evaluated

Benchmark construction has become explicit about what a world model must do beyond looking good. WorldModelBench contains "350 image and text condition pairs, ranging over 7 application driven domains, 56 diverse subdomains", uses crowd-sourced "67K human labels to accurately measure 14 frontier models", scores instruction-following in four levels and physics adherence as five binary physical-law violations, and reports that a fine-tuned 2B-parameter VLM judge achieves "8.6% higher avereage accuracy in predicting world modeling violations than GPT-4o" (the NeurIPS version states 9.9% lower error) [45]. The corresponding Hub dataset page is thin — 185 downloads in the last month, 34.2 MB total, "No dataset card yet", and a viewer that cannot detect supported data files — a reminder that benchmark artifacts are not always inspectable from their hosts [46].

Physics benchmarks give the sharpest numbers. Physics-IQ films each scenario "from 3 different angles" in a controlled environment and records it twice, so that physically realistic videos differing only in physical randomness would reach the maximum score of 100.0%, and it aggregates four metrics (Spatial IoU, Spatiotemporal IoU, Weighted-spatial IoU and MSE) [17][47]. The benchmark's own leaderboard lists VideoPoet multiframe 29.5%, Lumiere multiframe 23.0%, Runway Gen 3 i2v 22.8%, VideoPoet i2v 20.3%, Lumiere i2v 19.0%, Stable Video Diffusion 14.8%, Pika 13.0% and Sora i2v 10.0%, and notes that these values "are a bit higher than in an earlier version due to a change in the way the scores are normalized" — i.e. published scores are not stable across versions [17]. The paper's stated conclusion is that "physical understanding is severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism", and the camera-ready version presents visual realism and physics as separate axes evaluated independently [48][47].

WorldScore targets controllability of world generation, decomposing the problem "into a sequence of next-scene generation tasks with explicit camera trajectory-based layout specifications" over 3,000 test examples spanning static and dynamic, indoor and outdoor, photorealistic and stylized worlds, with controllability, quality and dynamics as metric families [49]. Its project page's result tables show where video models break: on camera control, 3D scene methods score 84.99–94.01 while video models such as VideoCrafter1-T2V 21.61, Hailuo 22.39 and Wan2.1 23.53; overall WorldScore-Static leaders are Voyager 77.62 and WonderWorld 72.69, with 4D-fy lowest at 27.98, and static 3D/4D methods are assigned 0 on the motion axes so that WorldScore-Dynamic "is not a direct ranking of overall capability" [50][51]. The official leaderboard is a running Hugging Face Gradio Space with 40 likes whose content is app-rendered and therefore only partially retrievable [52], and a third-party aggregator lists newer top entries (EonWorld 81.08 static / 73.37 dynamic, WorldScape-0.1 80.76, FantasyWorld-1.0 80.45) while cautioning that its rows are "Imported" [51].

Interaction-oriented benchmarks now dominate the recent evidence. WBench contains "289 test cases and 1,058 interaction turns" with five evaluation dimensions (video quality, setting adherence, interaction adherence, consistency, physics compliance), four interaction types, 22 automatic sub-metrics validated against human judgments, and the finding that across 20 state-of-the-art models "no single model performs strongly across all dimensions" [53]; its Hub dataset card exposes the schema (1–9 turns, six scene categories, first/third-person perspective) and a ranking with values such as 78.8, 78.4 and 78.2 for the top three entries [54]. WorldOlympiad decomposes evaluation into physical (segmentation plus MLLM-as-judge over mechanics, thermal phenomena and material properties), geometry (Gaussian-splatting reconstruction with cross-view and trajectory alignment) and interaction tracks spanning gaming, robotics and general real-world video, and reports "substantial gaps in physical reasoning, 3D consistency, and long-horizon interaction" [55]. WorldRoamBench tests "600+ test cases across Nature, Urban, and Indoor scenes in first/third-person views with WASD 10–60 s continuous interaction" using per-frame action metrics, segment-based drift detection, controllability-gated physics and action-decoupled memory, and finds that of 10+ models evaluated "none reliably satisfies all dimensions" [19]. PlayWorld replaces fixed action sequences with multi-modal agent players pursuing 171 specified objectives, because "the action sequence required to achieve the same objective may vary substantially between models"; across nine models it finds unreliable long-horizon behaviour, especially spatial consistency and persistent state evolution [20]. The third-party score summary shows Genie 3 best on overall rubric (2.12/3, with geometry 2.74 and out-of-sight 1.81) ahead of HappyOyster 1.92, while the model with the highest validation-pass rate (SANA-WM 80.4%) has modest rubric scores, "indicating it can follow trajectories without maintaining memory or spatial consistency"; failure modes include landmarks regenerated at new viewpoints and walking into water producing no expected physical response [21]. Domain benchmarks agree: DrivingGen, "the first comprehensive benchmark for generative driving world models" over 14 models, reports that "general models look better but break physics, while driving-specific ones capture motion realistically but lag in visual quality" [56], and Physion-Eval's "10,990 expert reasoning traces spanning 22 fine-grained physical categories" finds that "in physics-critical scenarios, 83.3% of exocentric and 93.5% of egocentric generated videos exhibit at least one human-identifiable physical glitch", criticizing prior work for relying on "automated metrics or coarse human judgments such as preferences or rubric-based checks" [18]. Physion-Eval's Hub card also shows the legal and methodological compromises involved: the first five frames of each generated video were removed "to reduce copyright risk", faces and brand marks are excluded, and the license permits academic non-commercial benchmarking but forbids generative-model training without explicit permission [57].

Crucially, several recent papers audit the benchmarks themselves. Physics-IQ Verified performs a "systematic audit of the Physics-IQ benchmark", refining "57.6% of all samples" and improving "over 34.8% of prompts", and finds that rankings shift "moderate but meaningful[ly]" (Kendall's τ = 0.46) under a sample-level scoring scheme, criticizing earlier practice that used "distributional metrics that compare unmatched sets of generated and real-world videos, such as Frechet Video Distance" [58]. WorldBench argues that existing physics tests "suffer from entanglement, where a single test simultaneously evaluates multiple physical laws and concepts", and in response evaluates single isolated concepts down to "low-level physical constants and material properties such as friction coefficients or fluid viscosity", finding "that all tested models lacking the physical consistency required to generate reliable real-world interactions" [59]. PDI-Bench lifts segmented, point-tracked objects "to 3D world-space coordinates via monocular reconstruction" and measures projective-geometry residuals for scale-depth alignment, 3D motion consistency and structural rigidity, revealing "consistent geometry-specific failure modes that are not captured by common perceptual metrics" [60]. Memory limits are also being measured directly: temporal RoPE offsets beyond the training horizon mean models "can no longer reliably address stored content once rollouts extend beyond the training horizon", naive cache compression "corrupts memory by averaging together incompatible positional phases", and the new LoopBench tests whether a compressed cache can reconstruct a previously visited scene after a long detour, with reported improvements of +15.5% temporal consistency and +19.5% episodic recall without retraining [61].

## Failure modes, exploitation and safety

The deepest criticism is not about pixels but about what imperfect models do to decision-making. A recent formal treatment defines model exploitation as the case where "a world model is exploitable if it implies that one policy should be strictly preferred over another while the environment's true transition model implies the reverse", proves that "exploitation is essentially unavoidable on large policy sets", shows that reward-hacking inevitability proofs "do not transfer to exploitation", and derives an ε-exploitability "safe horizon" for discounted MDPs in which "a less accurate model exhausts its error budget sooner, so safe planning requires a shorter horizon"; the authors emphasize the ordinal nature of the problem — "low prediction error does not preclude ordinal failures: a model can be accurate yet still invert the policy ordering induced by the true dynamics" — and note that Dreamer, MuZero and JEPA-style methods measure model quality through predictive accuracy or downstream performance, "both of which are cardinal" [22]. Remedies are being attempted: RENEW describes exploitation "ranging from agents that teleport through walls in a simulated maze to deployed systems that act on incorrect dynamics", uses a Bradley-Terry preference loss over trajectory likelihoods plus epistemic-uncertainty-guided queries, and reports that naive preference learning "requires an outsize preference budget" while prior robustness methods (MOPO, MOReL, COMBO) "trade coverage for robustness, limiting the ability of model-based methods to generalize beyond the training distribution" [62].

Security-focused work extends the same concern to agent pipelines: "attackers can induce mispredictions in agentic pipelines with up to 95% success rate, possibly resulting in unintended command execution, denial of service, drainage of wallet and private information extraction", with text world models underestimating program runtime in 95% of cases and 21% of samples not finishing within an hour [63]. Older imitation-learning results remain relevant: because behavioural cloning is "non-causal", "access to more information can yield worse performance" (causal misidentification), demonstrated in five benchmark control/Driving tasks, with fixes requiring targeted interventions and a disentangled state representation [64]. Safety-oriented reviews of embodied world models report concrete pathologies in their quantitative analyses: the review's own fault categorization reports that across 11 manually evaluated RoboGen tasks "physical consistency is violated in 45.5% of tasks", and that models "had poor positional awareness hence failed in tasks, and also collided with nearby objects"; it concludes that "while MLLM metrics can serve as a proxy for performance, human annotation remains the most reliable method for addressing pathologies of generated scenes" [65]. The Genie 3 discussion illustrates the resulting "reliability paradox": if generated worlds suffer physics inaccuracies, "how could such agents ever be fully reliable?" — with the researchers' response that simulations may demonstrate unreliability when an agent fails, even if they cannot guarantee correctness [66]. Qualitative web coverage also documents the horizon gap between generations, noting Genie 2's memory "topped out at around 10 seconds" versus Genie 3's minutes, and that GameNGen was low-resolution and limited to Doom [66].

The scaling question is itself disputed. A controlled 2D classical-mechanics study scaling a diffusion video model family from 30K to 3M examples and 22M to 310M parameters finds "near-perfect ID generalization" but that "OOD velocity errors are an order of magnitude higher than ID errors" (DiT-L on uniform motion: 0.427 vs. 0.012), and that "scaling up the training data and model size has little or negative impact on reducing this prediction error"; combinatorial generalization does improve (abnormal generations fall "from 67% to 10%"), yet the authors conclude that "naively scaling is insufficient for video generation models to discover fundamental physical laws" and that models exhibit "case-based" generalization with a referencing priority of "color >> size >> velocity >> shape" [23]. This is the empirical backing for the position paper that states flatly: "A world model (WM) is NOT about generating videos, but IS about simulating all possibilities of the world to serve as a sandbox for general-purpose reasoning via thought-experiments", proposing a Generative Latent Prediction architecture (Physical, Agentic, Nested) as an alternative [24]. At the same time, the broader survey literature notes that "the definition of a world model remains a subject of ongoing debate, generally divided into two primary perspectives: understanding the world and predicting the future", and that the field is "shifting focus from basic, user-uncontrolled video generation towards interactive simulations" [35].

## Applications beyond games: robotics, driving, medicine and Earth systems

Robotics is where world models are most directly coupled to action. A comprehensive robot-learning survey describes how world models have "progressed from imagination-based generation to controllable, structured, and foundation-scale formulations", serving both as learned simulators for RL and evaluation and as policy components, while stating that the literature "remains fragmented across architectures, functional roles, and embodied application domains" [67]. The concrete transfer evidence remains narrow but real: V-JEPA 2-AC plans zero-shot on Franka arms in two labs using 62 hours of unlabeled Droid video and image goals, with 65–80% reported pick-and-place success on new objects in unseen environments and a 100/60/20/80/50% profile on reach/grasp-cup/grasp-box/pick-cup/pick-box tasks [32][33][44]; Cosmos positions post-trained world models specifically for instruction-following manipulation and driving scenarios [10][25]; and multi-agent egocentric modelling (ME-World) is being formulated "as synchronized ego-stream generation for multiple agents interacting through fine-grained actions in a shared world", with the stated gap that prior multi-agent models "rely on coarse actions like locomotion, camera control, or discrete commands, leaving fine-grained embodied interactions underexplored" [68]. Driving follows the same generative logic, with GAIA-1/GAIA-2 and their catalogued peers aiming at controllable simulation of common and rare scenarios rather than direct control [42][43], and the DrivingGen benchmark showing that generative driving models currently trade physical plausibility against visual realism [56].

Outside control, two domains provide unusually concrete outcome evidence. In medicine, the Medical World Model (MeWM) is described as "the first world model in medicine that visually predicts future disease states based on clinical decisions": vision-language policy models generate treatment plans, tumour dynamics models simulate progression or regression, and an inverse dynamics model applies survival analysis to the simulated post-treatment tumour, "boost[ing] F1-score in selecting the optimal TACE protocol by 13%" with state-of-the-art specificity in Turing tests evaluated by radiologists and stronger performance than medical-specialized GPTs — against the backdrop that LLMs "diagnose significantly worse than clinicians and make less informed treatment decisions" and that world models were previously "largely underexplored in the medical domain" [69]. In Earth-system forecasting, Aurora is trained on "more than one million hours of diverse geophysical data" and reports outperforming operational forecasts at orders of magnitude lower compute: 5-day air-pollution forecasts at 0.4° beat numerical atmospheric chemistry simulations on 74% of targets, 10-day ocean-wave forecasts at 0.25° exceed costly numerical models on 86%, 5-day tropical cyclone track forecasts beat seven operational centres on 100%, and 10-day weather forecasts at 0.1° surpass state-of-the-art numerical models on 92%, with validation performance improving "approximately 6% for every ten times increase in model size" — a scaling relationship that stands in contrast to the video-physics scaling results; the authors also state that this timeline "is only possible because of the wealth of data that is available as a result of decades of research into traditional numerical approaches" [70].

## Trends and open problems

Four shifts recur across the retrieved corpus. First, the centre of gravity moved from latent dynamics to large-scale generative world foundation models — dedicated simulators such as Cosmos, interactive models such as Genie 2/3, and internet-video pre-training "comprising over 1 million hours" in the V-JEPA 2 case — with the explicit framing that these models are for "designing and evaluating" embodied agents rather than only for control [35][10][32][12]. Second, evaluation has professionalized: within roughly eighteen months the field produced multi-domain human-labelled benchmarks (WorldModelBench, 350 pairs / 67K labels) [45], physics-first suites with real-video normalization (Physics-IQ) [17][47], unified controllability benchmarks (WorldScore, 3,000 examples) [49], multi-turn interactive suites (WBench, 289 cases / 1,058 turns; WorldRoamBench, 600+ cases) [53][19], agent-player protocols (PlayWorld, 171 objectives) [20], and domain benchmarks (DrivingGen) [56]. Third, the evidence is increasingly negative and self-auditing: benchmark revisions move rankings (τ = 0.46) [58], physics tests are entangled [59], geometry failures escape perceptual metrics [60], and memory is bounded by positional-encoding horizons [61]. Fourth, application domains diversified, with robotics surveys formalizing the taxonomy [71][67] and medicine and Earth system science supplying measurable outcomes [69][70].

The unresolved problems are equally consistent across sources. Long-horizon consistency and error accumulation are named as central: one-step prediction errors "can get magnified", and long rollouts suffer "error accumulation, which establishes coherence as a central challenge in video prediction and policy imagination" [71]; a survey of embodied world models additionally identifies "the scarcity of unified datasets and the need for evaluation metrics that assess physical consistency over pixel fidelity" and "the trade-off between model performance and the computational efficiency required for real-time control" [71]. Interactive models remain bounded in time and control: Genie 3 supports "a few minutes" rather than hours, with limited action space and no real-world geographic accuracy [16][66], and NVIDIA's own authors state that "the world foundation model problem is still far from being solved" [25]. Whether generative video can constitute physical understanding stays disputed: the Sora-affirmative reading coexists with the flat counter-claim that world modelling "IS NOT about generating videos" [24][36], and with a position-paper-style critique that data, representation, architecture, learning objective and usage remain open design questions [24]. Data remains a bottleneck for embodied applications, since Physical AI requires interleaved observation-action sequences whose exploratory actions "may cause severe damage" [25][10]. Formal guarantees are missing: exploitation is unavoidable on large policy sets, the safe horizon shrinks with error, and "there are, as of yet, no known closed-form necessary and sufficient conditions that guarantee unexploitability in finite policy sets", with results restricted to finite-state MDPs [22]. On the tooling side, the label remains unstable, with Hub artifacts spanning 0.1B–8B parameters of RL agents, video-diffusion dynamics and LLM world-state models [26], leaderboards split between papers, Spaces and third-party aggregators [52][51][54], and no unified sim-to-real transfer measurement across world-model families in the retrieved evidence [71][65].
